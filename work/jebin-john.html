The situation

A moment with a short shelf life.

Jebin John appeared on a Netflix reality series and became one of the most talked-about names of the season. Attention like that arrives all at once and leaves just as fast — and the default outcome is a spike in followers that decays within weeks because nothing is built to catch it.

There was no content system underneath the moment: no shooting schedule, no editor, no publishing calendar, no plan for the comment sections that were about to fill up, and no commercial pipeline to convert attention into revenue.

Apex Media was brought in to build and run that system, end to end, rather than advise on it.

What we ran

Six workstreams, one team.

On-set recording

We filmed. Day-of direction, multicam capture, wireless audio, and asset handoff straight into the edit queue — so the content plan actually got shot instead of discussed.

Daily editing

Every clip cut in-house: hook-first structure, burned-in captions, motion graphics, and sound design tuned for a muted autoplay first second.

Account management

Four platforms run as one calendar — Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and Snapchat — with three pieces of content shipped per day and no dark days.

Community management

Comment sections and DMs worked actively rather than monitored passively, including deliberate engagement strategy during a high-volume backlash cycle.

Brand partnerships

Media kit, outbound pipeline to consumer and fitness brands, and negotiation support on inbound partnership offers.

Reporting

Weekly performance review across all four platforms, with the next batch of content shaped by what the last batch actually did.

The pivot

Turning a television moment into an asset.

The televised storyline generated an enormous volume of commentary — much of it hostile. Rather than damage-control it, we treated the comment sections as distribution: replies became content, the volume became reach, and the cycle fed the publishing calendar instead of derailing it.

In parallel we packaged the momentum commercially — a media kit, outbound brand partnership pipeline, and a casting pitch delivered to the production company behind the series for a companion show.

The stack

What we ran it on.

Owned equipment and a fixed pipeline, so output stayed consistent whether we were shooting in studio or on a street.

  • Sony mirrorless body for primary capture, HDMI capture card for live
  • SM7B microphone into a Rodecaster Pro for studio-grade voiceover
  • DJI wireless mic system for run-and-gun and man-on-the-street
  • Restream + Streamlabs for simultaneous multi-platform live
  • Voiceover-first edit workflow so visuals sync to speaker pacing
  • Central asset library with labelled, backed-up raw footage
